The restaurant registration process in Nepal has six steps: register the business, take a PAN from the Inland Revenue Department, register the outlet at your ward office, apply for the food licence from the Department of Food Technology and Quality Control, take the operating licence from your municipality, and get the fire-safety and health clearances. Most of it can be done in a fortnight if your documents are in order; the food licence is usually the one that decides how long it takes.

This is a practical summary for owners, not legal advice. Requirements differ between a ward in Kathmandu and one in Pokhara, and they change. Confirm each step with the office that issues it.

1. Register the business

Two common routes:

  • Sole proprietorship (firm): registered at the local ward office or the Department of Cottage and Small Industries. Cheaper, quicker, and the owner is personally liable.
  • Private limited company: registered at the Office of the Company Registrar. More paperwork and an audit obligation, but liability is limited and it is easier to take a partner in later.

If two or more people are putting money in, do the company route and write down who owns what before the first rupee is spent.

2. Take your PAN

A Permanent Account Number from the Inland Revenue Department is required for every business. Restaurants and bars also sit in the group that has to register for VAT rather than waiting for a turnover threshold, so expect to come away registered for both. What that changes about your bills is covered in PAN bill and VAT bill in Nepal.

Ward registration and PAN can often be finished on the same day if you go with everything in hand.

3. Register at the ward office

The ward wants to know that this business exists at this address. Take the business registration certificate, your PAN certificate, citizenship copies and the rent agreement. A sketch or photo of the premises is sometimes asked for.

4. The DFTQC food licence

Any business making or selling food needs a licence from the Department of Food Technology and Quality Control under the Food Act. Applications go through the NeFFILS portal (neffils.dftqc.gov.np) or the DFTQC office covering your district.

Expect to provide the business registration certificate, PAN, citizenship copies, the lease or ownership proof, a layout of the kitchen, and health certificates for food handlers. An inspection of the premises is part of it, so have the kitchen clean, the storage off the floor and a hand-wash point before you invite anyone.

5. The municipal operating licence

Your municipality or rural municipality issues the licence that actually lets the shutter open, usually renewed every year. Some wards fold this into step 3; others treat it separately. Ask which yours does, because the renewal date is easy to miss and the penalty is annoying rather than small.

6. Fire safety and health clearances

A commercial kitchen with gas needs fire-safety sign-off, and bigger places are asked for a sanitation or health clearance. Both are cheaper to satisfy while the kitchen is being built than after. Extinguishers near the stove line, a clear exit path, and a gas bank that is outside and ventilated cover most of what is asked.

7. If you serve alcohol

A liquor licence is separate, issued under the relevant excise rules through the district administration or the municipality depending on the category. It takes longer than the rest, costs more, and renews yearly. Start it early rather than at the end.

9. The documents to keep in one folder

  • Business registration certificate
  • PAN and VAT certificates
  • Ward registration
  • DFTQC food licence and its renewal dates
  • Municipal operating licence
  • Fire-safety and health clearances
  • Lease agreement
  • Food handlers' health certificates

Scan every one of them the day you receive it. Inspections ask for the folder, not the story, and a renewal you forgot is the most avoidable fine in this business. Once the paperwork is done, the till is what keeps you compliant day to day: bills numbered in order within the fiscal year, VAT shown separately, and a copy of every bill kept. Common questions

How long does the whole thing take? Two to six weeks is typical. The food licence and, if you need one, the liquor licence set the pace.

Can I open while the food licence is still being processed? Do not plan on it. Opening without it is a risk that costs more than the few days you would save.

Do I need VAT registration for a small momo shop? Restaurants are in the group expected to register regardless of turnover, though advisers differ on how small an eatery has to be before that stops applying. One phone call to your tax office settles it for your case.

Is a cloud kitchen treated differently? It still needs the business registration, PAN, ward registration and a DFTQC food licence. What changes is the premises inspection, which looks at a production kitchen rather than a dining room.

What does it cost? Government fees for a small place commonly come to a few tens of thousands of rupees in total. The bigger numbers are in the kitchen and the deposit — see the cost to open a restaurant in Nepal.
