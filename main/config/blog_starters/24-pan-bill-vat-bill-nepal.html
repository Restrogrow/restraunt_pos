A PAN bill is an ordinary sales bill issued by a business that is registered for a Permanent Account Number but not for VAT. A VAT bill — properly, a tax invoice — is issued by a VAT-registered business, adds 13% VAT, and is the only one a buyer can use to claim that VAT back. Most restaurants in Nepal end up issuing VAT bills, because restaurants are one of the businesses that have to register for VAT.

This is general information for restaurant owners, not tax advice. Thresholds and formats change with each year's finance act, so confirm the current position with your tax office or accountant before you act on it.

1. The difference between a PAN bill and a VAT bill

PAN billVAT bill (tax invoice)
Who issues itA business registered for PAN onlyA business registered for VAT
VAT on the billNone13%, shown separately
Buyer can claim the VATNoYes, if the buyer is VAT-registered
Counts as a business expenseUsually yesYes
Typical issuerSmall shop, consultant, small supplierRestaurant, hotel, bar, most traders

The thing people miss: a PAN bill is not a lesser or informal bill. It is a real bill for tax purposes. The only difference is that no VAT is charged on it, so no VAT can be claimed from it.

2. What a PAN bill must show

  • Your business name, address and PAN
  • A bill number that runs in order, without gaps, and the date
  • The buyer's name (and PAN, where the buyer wants to claim the expense)
  • What was sold, the quantity and the rate
  • The total, in figures and usually in words

Keep a copy of every bill. Numbering that skips, restarts at random or gets handwritten over is the single most common finding when an officer looks at a small business's books.

3. What a VAT bill must show, on top of that

  • The words tax invoice
  • Your VAT registration number (in Nepal this is your PAN, used as the VAT number)
  • The taxable value and the 13% VAT as two separate lines, not one merged total
  • The buyer's name, address and PAN when the buyer is VAT-registered
  • A copy kept for your own records — the "copy of original bill" the rules refer to

A restaurant bill usually has one more line between the food and the VAT: the service charge, which is added before VAT is worked out. The order matters, and getting it wrong changes the total.

Service charge is not a tax and is not collected on behalf of the government; it is part of what you charge, which is why VAT applies on top of it. If you do not levy a service charge, the taxable value is simply the food total.

5. Which one does a restaurant issue?

Almost always a VAT bill, because restaurants are not left to the usual turnover test. In Nepal a business must register for VAT once annual turnover passes the Inland Revenue Department's threshold — broadly Rs 50 lakh for goods and Rs 30 lakh for services or a mix of both — but a list of specified businesses has to register from the first sale whatever the turnover, and restaurants and bars are on that list. Advisers differ on whether every small eatery falls inside it or only those serving alcohol, so this is worth one phone call to your tax office rather than a guess.

If you are registered for PAN only today and expect to cross the line, register before you cross it. Registering late means VAT is still owed on sales you already made at prices that did not include it, and you pay it out of your margin.

6. The PAN bill limit people ask about

There is no single "PAN bill limit" that caps what one bill can be worth. What people usually mean is one of two things: the turnover at which you must move from PAN-only to VAT registration, or the amount above which a buyer must have a proper bill to claim the expense. Neither is a limit on the bill itself. If your supplier tells you they "cannot give a bill above a certain amount", that is a sign they are not registered for what they are selling you.

Common questions

Is a PAN bill valid for claiming expenses? Yes. It proves the purchase. What it does not do is give the buyer any VAT to claim, because none was charged.

Can I issue a PAN bill if I am VAT-registered? No. Once you are registered for VAT, your sales bills are tax invoices with VAT shown.

What is the difference between a VAT bill and an abbreviated tax invoice? The abbreviated form is allowed for small retail sales to ordinary customers and leaves out some buyer details. A buyer who wants to claim the VAT needs the full tax invoice, so ask customers at the table whether they need a bill in a company's name before you print it.

Do I need to print the bill, or is a digital copy enough? Give the customer a bill. Keep your own copy — on paper or in the software, as long as it cannot be quietly edited later.

What happens if I do not issue bills? Penalties, and in a restaurant it also means you have no reliable figure for your own sales. Owners who start billing every sale properly are often surprised by what the numbers show about their busiest hours and their worst-selling dishes.
