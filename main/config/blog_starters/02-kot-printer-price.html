2. What decides the price

Paper width: 80 mm printers cost more than 58 mm ones, but kitchen tickets are much easier to read on 80 mm paper. For the kitchen, choose 80 mm.

Connection: USB is cheapest but the printer must sit next to the computer. LAN (network cable) lets the printer sit in the kitchen while billing happens at the counter or on waiters' phones. Bluetooth is wireless but only works over short distances and can disconnect in a busy kitchen.

Auto-cutter: cuts each KOT automatically. Worth the extra ₹1,000–₹2,000 in a kitchen, because cooks won't tear slips by hand.

Print speed: faster printers (200–250 mm per second) matter only when many orders come at once.

Build and warranty: kitchens are hot, oily and humid. A 1-year warranty with a local service centre is worth paying for.

3. Which KOT printer should you buy?

  • Cart, kiosk or tiny cafe: a 58 mm Bluetooth printer connected to your phone is enough.
  • Small restaurant with one kitchen: one 80 mm LAN printer with auto-cutter in the kitchen, plus one at the counter for bills.
  • Restaurant with tandoor, Chinese and main kitchen: one LAN printer per station, so each station prints only its own items.
  • Cloud kitchen: 80 mm LAN printer at the packing counter; consider a kitchen screen instead of a second printer.

Before buying, check that your billing software supports the printer's connection type. RestroGrow works with USB, network (LAN) and Bluetooth thermal printers.

4. Running costs

Thermal printers use no ink, only heat-sensitive paper rolls. An 80 mm roll usually costs ₹30 to ₹60 depending on length and quality, bought in boxes of 50 or 100. A restaurant printing 150 KOTs a day typically uses a few rolls a week. Buy good-quality rolls: cheap paper fades within weeks and leaves dust that damages the print head.

5. Do you need a printer at all?

Not always. A tablet on the kitchen wall running a kitchen display can replace the KOT printer: orders appear on screen, cooks tap "ready", and nothing gets lost. Many restaurants keep one printer as a backup. Read more in our guide to how KOT works in restaurants.

FAQ

What is the price of an 80 mm thermal printer in India?

Usually ₹4,000 to ₹9,000. Models with LAN, Wi-Fi and auto-cutter from big brands can cost ₹10,000 or more.

Is a Bluetooth printer good for a restaurant kitchen?

It is fine for a small cafe or cart. In a busy kitchen, a LAN printer is more reliable because Bluetooth can disconnect and has a short range.

Can the same printer print KOT and bills?

Yes, but it gets confusing during rush hours. Most restaurants use one printer at the counter for bills and one in the kitchen for KOTs.

How long does a thermal printer last?

A good one lasts several years. Keep it away from water and oil, use good paper, and clean the print head every few weeks.
